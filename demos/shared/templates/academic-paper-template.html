Seasonal stage anomalies along the central Amazon, mapped from fourteen gauging stations

Marisol Quintana1,*, Tobias Hartwell2, Ilse Brandvold1 and Kofi Adjei-Lindqvist2

1 Institute for Fluvial Geomorphology, University of Vale Branco
2 Centre for Hydrographic Survey, Northern Rivers Academy · * Correspondence: m.quintana@example.org

Abstract

We present monthly stage anomalies for fourteen illustrative gauging stations on the Solimões, Negro and Amazon rivers around Manaus, and show how a diverging colour scale makes the lagged flood pulse legible on a single map. Anomalies peak first on the upper Solimões and arrive at the lower Amazon roughly six weeks later, while the Negro responds with a damped, delayed signal. An interactive figure lets readers step through the hydrological year and inspect any station.

Keywords: stage anomaly; flood pulse; gauging network; diverging colour scale; Amazon basin

Introduction

Water levels on the central Amazon rise and fall by more than ten metres each year, yet the departures from the long-term mean are what matter for navigation and floodplain agriculture. Mapping those departures, rather than absolute stage, removes the dominant seasonal cycle and exposes anomalous years at a glance.

Earlier atlases show stations as proportional symbols on a continental base. Here we colour each station by its anomaly in the selected month (Fig. 1) and give the full annual trace on demand (Fig. 2). Station metadata are listed in Table 1.

Study area and data

The network spans about 330 km of the central Amazon, from Novo Airão on the lower Negro to Itapiranga on the Amazon main stem1. The reach includes the confluence near Manaus, where dark Negro water meets the sediment-laden Solimões and the two flow side by side for tens of kilometres.

Daily stage readings were reduced to monthly means and expressed as departures from a twenty-year reference mean at each station. Positive values indicate stage above the reference.

Methods

Discharge at a station is related to stage through Manning’s equation for uniform open-channel flow,

Q = 1n A R2/3S1/2
(1)

where Q is discharge, n the roughness coefficient, A the flow area, R the hydraulic radius and S the energy slope. Because A and R both grow with stage, small stage anomalies translate into disproportionately large discharge anomalies.2

Anomalies are encoded with a colour-blind-safe diverging scale, red for below-reference and blue for above-reference stage, centred on a neutral light grey so that near-normal stations recede.

2 Roughness is held constant across the reach.

Discussion

The lag between the western and eastern stations is consistent with a flood wave travelling at roughly 0.5 m s−1. Because the diverging scale is anchored at zero, a single glance at Fig. 1 separates anomalously wet from anomalously dry reaches without reading a number.

Limitations include the short reference period and the assumption of a stable rating curve. Future work will extend the network to the Madeira and Purus tributaries.
